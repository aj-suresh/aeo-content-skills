# The Edge Gate

This is the step that decides whether the brief exists. Everything else in the pipeline is craft; this is judgment.

## Why It's a Hard Stop

The failure mode of AI-assisted content is not bad writing. It's *competent, well-structured, entirely redundant* writing — pages that read fine and add nothing. Those pages don't rank, don't get cited, and cost real money to produce.

The edge gate is the only step that can catch this before the draft. Once a draft exists, sunk cost takes over and it gets published.

## The Question

**What do the top 5 results NOT do?**

Answer it from the coverage map, not from imagination. If you skipped or rushed the SERP research, you cannot answer this honestly and the gate is meaningless.

## The Four Valid Edges

Pick exactly one. Two edges usually means neither is real.

### 1. Original data
A number only this company can produce — from their product, their customers, their operations.

- ✅ "Median time-to-first-deploy across 12,000 repos on our platform, split by team size"
- ✅ "What we learned migrating 60 customers off legacy billing systems last year, including the two that failed"
- ❌ "Industry statistics on developer productivity" — that's everyone else's data, and it's already in the top 5

**Test:** could a competitor produce this exact number? If yes, it isn't original data.

### 2. Specific ICP
Written for a segment the top results address generically or ignore.

- ✅ "For 2-person ops teams, who can't execute the enterprise playbooks the top results assume"
- ✅ "For DTC brands under $1M GMV, where the standard advice about warehouse automation is actively wrong"
- ❌ "For business professionals" — that's who the top 5 are already writing for

**Test:** does the specificity change the *advice*, or just the intro paragraph? If the recommendations are identical and only the framing narrowed, it's not an edge.

### 3. Named framework
A repeatable model this page defines, names, and teaches.

- ✅ "The Activation Ladder: five stages from signup to habit, with the intervention that moves someone up each rung"
- ❌ "A step-by-step guide" — steps are not a framework

**Test:** could someone cite the framework *by name* six months later? A framework people can name is an asset; a numbered list is content.

### 4. Deeper subtopic
The thing every top result mentions in one line and none explain.

- ✅ "Everyone says 'clean your data first.' Nobody says what clean means, which fields matter, or how long it actually takes."
- ❌ "More detail throughout" — that's length, not depth

**Test:** can you point at the specific sentence in a competitor's page that this expands? If you can't cite the one-liner you're deepening, you haven't found the gap.

## The Forcing Sentence

Complete it, out loud, with no hedging:

> **"This page does {X}, which no one else in the top 5 does."**

Rejected completions — every one of these is a fail:

| Completion | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "...covers the topic more thoroughly" | Length isn't an edge |
| "...is more up to date" | Only an edge if the topic actually changed; usually it hasn't |
| "...is better written" | Unfalsifiable, and probably untrue |
| "...includes original data" | Name the data or it doesn't exist |
| "...is from our unique perspective" | Perspective isn't an edge; a specific claim is |
| "...targets a more specific audience" | Name the audience and show the advice changes |

## When the Gate Fails

**Single mode — stop. Do not write the brief.**

Say it plainly:

> The brief isn't ready. Every top-5 result already does what this page would do, and I can't find a gap worth publishing into.
>
> Three ways forward: **find real data** you can put behind this, **narrow to a segment** where the standard advice actually breaks down, or **brief a different angle** — often the deeper subtopic inside this one is the better page.

Then help find it. Ask what they know that isn't public:

- What do customers ask in sales calls that the top 5 don't answer?
- What do support tickets cluster around?
- What number could their product report that nobody else has?
- What advice do they give privately that contradicts the public consensus?

Do not write a brief with an empty edge field to be accommodating. That brief becomes a draft, the draft becomes a published page, and the published page does nothing. Stopping here is the entire value of the step.

**Batch mode — mark, don't halt.**

Write the brief with `STATUS: BLOCKED — no edge` and a filled H2 map. The user gets a triage pile: briefs that are ready, and briefs that need an edge before anyone writes them. Report the split in your summary.

## A Note on Honesty

You will be tempted to accept a weak edge because the user seems invested and stopping feels unhelpful. Resist it. The user asked for a brief that ranks and gets cited. A brief with a fake edge doesn't do either, and telling them the truth now is worth more than a document that wastes a writer's week.
